Organizing Literature

After identifying a set of relevant articles through Scopus, the next step is to organize the literature in a meaningful way. At this stage, the goal is not simply to read articles one by one, but to begin understanding how different studies relate to each other.

You should now have a set of candidate articles, typically around 10 or more. These articles need to be read strategically so that you can identify their key ideas, methods, and findings. By doing this, you will start to see patterns across the literature: where studies support each other, where they extend existing work, and where they present different perspectives.

To support this process, it is helpful to create structured notes for each article and to organize the information in a clear overview. The goal is not only to summarize individual studies, but to build a foundation for identifying the main themes, debates, and logical structure that will later shape your literature review.

The content below is based on course materials in Canvas. The reading instructions provide guidance on how to analyze scientific articles efficiently, while the literature overview template helps you organize and compare the articles you have selected.

Annex VIII: Required Reading Instructions for Scientific Articles

General Questions

  • What is the article's central question?
  • What is the article's conclusion/What is the answer to its central question?
  • Which problem is discussed? What is its relevance?
  • What theories and other studies form the basis of the research question?
  • What are the central concepts of the article and how are these defined and operationalised?
  • What is the basis for the article's conclusion? Is it someone's own research (see question 7), or an analysis of an existing study or literary source (see question 15)?
  • If it is based on research, what kind of research are we talking about? (questionnaire survey, interview, observations, experiments, etc.)
  • What are the dependent and independent variables of the study?
  • What are the characteristics of a sample (number of participants, sample make-up)?
  • How was the study conducted?
  • What data/results were generated by the study?
  • What do the results mean?
  • What is the relationship between the results generated and the conclusions?
  • If the conclusion is based on literature, what are the main findings in the literature that could be used to support the conclusion?

Assessing an article for relevance to your own question

  • Does this article relate to your own central question? To what extent does it contribute to answering that question? Why is the article relevant?

Critical questions

  • To what extent do you feel that the article's findings support its conclusions?
  • What evidence is presented to support this conclusion?
  • Do you feel that the evidence is sound and accurate?
  • Do you feel that the evidence is convincing, relevant and complete?
  • Has the evidence been interpreted correctly?
  • Does the author make any claims that are not supported by evidence?
  • In what way is the article's content related to what you already know about the subject?

Using GenAI in AWSI

Formulating a Literature Overview Table

This step focuses on translating your reading into a structured overview of the literature. After selecting relevant articles, your task is to read each paper and extract its key components into the literature overview table. The purpose is not to produce perfect summaries, but to build a clear, organized "top view" of your sources. This overview serves as the foundation for later stages, where you will compare studies, identify patterns, and construct your argument. At this stage, the priority is to capture your understanding in a structured form, not to refine or evaluate it.

GenAI in this activity functions as a language support tool, not as a source of content. The informational process should remain: paper -> student -> (GenAI) -> table. You are responsible for understanding the article and deciding what information is relevant. GenAI supports you by helping you express that understanding more clearly, efficiently, and in an academically appropriate way. This reduces friction in writing and allows you to stay focused on the thinking process rather than wording or formatting. It helps you elaborate incomplete ideas, refine phrasing, and maintain flow, but it does not replace reading, interpretation, or decision making.
