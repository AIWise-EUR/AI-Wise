Finalizing the Paper

At this stage, the goal is to finalize your paper by checking how well it meets the rubric. Rather than focusing only on improving language or making the text sound better, you review your work systematically to identify whether any important requirements are still missing or insufficiently developed.

If a clear issue is identified, you are expected to return to the relevant earlier step, gather or refine what is needed, and then come back to check again. This page supports an iterative process of checking and revising, helping you focus on the most important improvements before submission.

Literature Report Review criteria

The final version of your literature report will be reviewed according to the rubric below.

Scale: 1 = unsatisfactory, 2 = mediocre, 3 = satisfactory, 4 = good, 5 = very good

Using GenAI in AWSI

Final Check of your paper using the Rubric

In this activity, you go through your paper using the rubric as a checklist. Instead of trying to improve everything at once, you focus on one criterion at a time and check whether your paper sufficiently meets that requirement.

With GenAI, the goal is not to rewrite your work, but to support this checking process. For each criterion, you provide the relevant part of your paper and ask GenAI to determine whether a substantial issue is present. If a clear issue is identified, GenAI helps you understand why it matters and suggests possible revision directions. These may include returning to earlier steps such as exploring the topic, refining your draft structure, or revising specific sections of your writing.

Importantly, GenAI's feedback is based on general patterns learned from large amounts of text. This means it is designed to produce responses that are typically reasonable or commonly acceptable, but not necessarily the most precise or context-specific judgment for your paper. For this reason, the final evaluation should be based on a combination of your own understanding of the rubric and GenAI's feedback. You are expected to compare both and decide whether a revision is needed and what kind of revision is most appropriate.

As a result, GenAI is particularly useful for detecting clear and substantial issues, rather than refining your paper to the highest possible level. For more nuanced improvements and higher-level quality decisions, it is recommended to discuss your work with peers, tutors, or the course coordinator.
